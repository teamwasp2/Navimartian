Model notes

Traverse time, battery, and oxygen use a pressurized-rover force balance, not a fixed speed. Mars gravity is 3.72 m/s². The loaded rover is 2,500 kg. Rolling resistance is Crr = 0.085 + 0.14 × dust, so soft regolith sinks the wheels. The drivetrain is 82% efficient. Downhill, when gravity exceeds rolling resistance, 35% of that mechanical energy is recovered by regenerative braking. Cabin life support and thermal control draw a continuous 1.8 kW. Flat-ground speed is 2.4 m/s and is cut by dust, grade, and a 10 kW traction limit. Two crew consume 0.18 kg/h of oxygen on the flat, more on climbs and in dust. Map distance stays the horizontal step; time and energy use the true 3D path. A* still routes on slope, radiation, and dust. Grade percent is rise over run on the real east-west and north-south spacing, and the drawn route is laid back onto that surface. A dust storm closes its whole dome, outer ring included, and both corridors stay outside it. A solar particle event adds up to 2.8 mSv/day on every cell, slightly less where the air column is thicker, without editing the terrain file.

Guide & Sources

  1. Mission sites. Use the top bar to switch Jezero Crater, Valles Marineris, Arcadia Planitia, Gale Crater, and Olympus Mons. Each load pulls that site’s terrain and snaps the beacons to its default sortie.
  2. Waypoints. Left-click the terrain to set the start base. Shift+click, or turn on "Set destination" and click, to place the target.
  3. Corridors and presets. "Safest" draws the solid cyan tube and is the plan to fly when it scores higher. "Fastest" draws the magenta dashed line beside it. "Custom" uses the weight sliders. "Heavy Cargo" sets a 15% slope limit. "Light Scout" sets a 25% limit and weights radiation more heavily.
  4. Overlays. "Surface", "Slope", and "Rad / dust" change the drape. Vertical exaggeration only stretches the picture. Slope % stays the true ground grade.
  5. Contingencies. "Trigger Dust Storm" drops a local dome. The whole dome is impassable, and the status line reports when both corridors stay outside it. "Relocate Dust Storm" moves that dome. "Trigger Solar Storm (SEP)" raises radiation across the whole region. Both can be on together.
  6. Playback and export. "Simulate EVA traverse" drives the rover on the physics clock. "Export Mission Plan (.json)" downloads the site, hazards, comparison, and lat/lon waypoints.

Radiation

Anchored to NASA Curiosity RAD surface measurements, about 0.64 mSv/day at Gale and Jezero. The rate rises where the air column is thinner, and a solar particle event adds up to 2.8 mSv/day, slightly less in lower terrain. Dose is that rate times the hours driven, divided by 24.

Dust and terramechanics

Dust slip is higher on flat, low basins. The field follows the pattern in Perseverance MEDA dust optical depth and Mars Odyssey THEMIS thermal inertia: soft ground raises rolling resistance, Crr = 0.085 + 0.14 × dust.

Rover physics and life support

Mars gravity is 3.72 m/s². The pressurized rover is 2,500 kg, on the NASA Space Exploration Vehicle scale. The drivetrain is 82% efficient, downhill regeneration returns 35%, and the cabin draws a continuous 1.8 kW.

Pathfinding

Eight-direction A* on true ground spacing. Step cost is meters times slope, radiation, and dust. Cells steeper than the slope limit, and every cell inside the dust-storm dome, are impassable.
